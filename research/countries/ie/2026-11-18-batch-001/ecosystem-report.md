## Country and measurement boundaries

The user assigned Ireland to GDP cohort **27/30**, using IMF October 2026 WEO nominal GDP estimates for 2026, excluding aggregates. That is the sampling instruction, not a GDP finding made by this packet. The supplied [DataMapper API](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/api/v1/NGDPD/2026) returned `DisabledError` through the web reader. The [WEO landing page](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/datasets/WEO) exposed a template shell rather than the requested edition and values. GDP amount and independent rank verification remain unknown. No final-actual GDP claim is made.

The country scope is the **Republic of Ireland**. Irish origins, current headquarters, operating offices and legal incorporation are separate attributes. Northern Ireland is not silently included in the country count. Where a national-looking source actually covers the island of Ireland, its geographic scope is retained. Overseas-headquartered, dual-headquartered, acquired and renamed Irish-origin businesses are useful ecosystem evidence, but must not be counted indiscriminately as current independent Ireland-headquartered startups.

This first checkpoint deliberately covers three research lanes: software and fintech; medical technology and life sciences; and deep technology, industrial AI, robotics and energy. It is a purposive sample with a named, resumable queue. It is not a representative survey, a national census or a complete funding history. Exact company, source, claim and disposition counts are in `manifest.json` and `coverage.json`.

## What the current evidence says

Ireland has a connected set of national public supports, university commercialisation routes, regional hubs and transatlantic investors. The practical research unit is therefore a company plus its evidence and organisational relationships, rather than a flat list labelled “Irish”. A founder's nationality, a Dublin office, a university relationship or participation in an accelerator alone does not settle headquarters or incorporation.

Enterprise Ireland's [7 May 2026 release](https://www.enterprise-ireland.com/en/news/over-32-million-invested-in-irish-startups-in-2025) reports **198 startups supported in 2025**, comprising 90 High Potential Start-Ups and 108 earlier-stage businesses, with **€32.9 million** invested. These are the agency's supported companies for one year. They are neither the population of active Irish startups nor a denominator against which this small packet can claim percentage completeness.

The latest opened [IVCA release, dated 15 November 2026](https://www.ivca.ie/third-quarter-venture-capital-funding-falls-to-lowest-level-in-ten-years-ivca/), reports **€692.2 million across 129 deals in January–September 2026**. Its Q3 figure is €113.8 million, down 45% from Q3 2025. The source identifies life sciences, fintech, software and cybersecurity as the leading sectors over those nine months. This is equity fundraising by Irish SMEs and other SMEs headquartered on the island; debt, venture debt, grants and other non-equity instruments are excluded. It is not a full-year 2026 or Republic-only startup statistic. The [2025 release](https://www.ivca.ie/annual-vc-funding-into-irish-smes-fell-for-first-time-since-2018/) provides the historical comparison of approximately €1.1 billion and 186 deals, using its own survey methodology.

An inference from these bounded sources is that Ireland's discovery strategy should combine globally oriented software businesses with university and clinical spinouts. A Dublin-only or well-known-unicorn-only pass would miss important routes into the ecosystem. That inference does not establish the relative number of startups in each city or sector.

## Programmes and public support

### Startup Ireland and the accelerator transition

[Startup Ireland was launched on 13 October 2026](https://www.enterprise-ireland.com/en/news/enterprise-ireland-launches-startup-ireland). Its roadmap announces a national hub, accelerator pathways, mentoring and international connections, with a 2026–2030 ambition to support 1,000 new startups. These are policy ambitions and announced initiatives, not already achieved company counts. Enterprise Ireland had announced a €21 million, three-year national accelerator budget in May 2026.

The [14 October AI accelerator announcement](https://www.enterprise-ireland.com/en/news/ei-launches-startup-ireland-ai-accelerator-programme) identifies Dogpatch Labs as operator and describes a 12-week, non-equity programme for ten selected companies. It names technology and investor networks. Membership in either network does not prove that a partner has invested in a participating startup. Potential credits are not cash raised, and stated programme capacity is not a verified cohort roster.

NDRC requires temporal care. Its [November 2024 statement](https://www.ndrc.ie/press-statement) announced an end in November 2025, yet the currently opened site has an [Accelerator 2026 cohort](https://www.ndrc.ie/accelerator-cohort-2026). A later government extension announcement dated 17 June 2025 was found in search, but opening that government page and its RTE corroboration returned 403. The registry preserves these outcomes; their search excerpts are not promoted into reviewed claims. Consequently the old closure statement is retained as a historical conflict, not used to classify NDRC as currently closed.

For applications, the directly opened [NDRC accelerator page](https://www.ndrc.ie/accelerator) explicitly says applications are now closed. It also describes a €100,000 uncapped SAFE with a 20% discount. That current notice takes precedence over its generic always-on marketing language. The displayed investment terms do not prove that every cohort company has received both instalments, and this packet creates no automatic funding events from cohort membership.

### Founder development and research commercialisation

[New Frontiers](https://www.newfrontiers.ie/about/faq) takes no equity and describes a conditional €17,500 allowance during Phase 2. Recruitment dates vary by delivery location. Programme participation is founder-development evidence, not venture equity.

[BioInnovate](https://www.universityofgalway.ie/bioinnovate/) is a ten-month medical-device and digital-health innovation programme based at University of Galway. The opened page explicitly says its 2026 applications are closed. Its clinical-needs pathway is a valuable discovery source for future medical-device companies, but a fellowship, project, spinout and incorporated company are different stages.

[Tyndall's Explorer page](https://www.tyndall.ie/explorer/) currently describes the 2027 three-month semiconductor commercialisation programme and says applications are open. The search result initially described the 2026 programme; the opened page is the preferred current observation. Eligibility covers research teams across the island of Ireland, including teams without an established company; it is broader than this packet's Republic scope. No precise deadline is asserted because one was not extracted.

### Pre-seed finance and sector programmes

The official [Pre-Seed Start Fund overview](https://www.enterprise-ireland.com/en/supports/pre-seed-start-fund) lists €50,000 or €100,000 investment options. The [National Enterprise Hub](https://www.neh.gov.ie/business-supports/pre-seed-start-fund) identifies the instrument as a convertible loan note and outlines MVP, validation, innovation and international-growth conditions. This is not an unconditional grant or evidence that an individual company received funding.

[NovaUCD's AI Ecosystem Accelerator](https://www.ucd.ie/innovation/start-ups/aiecosystemaccelerator/), operated with CeADAR under EDIH, is a separate programme from the new Startup Ireland AI accelerator. Its 2026 applications are closed, and the observed criteria refer to Irish-registered companies with validated products or pilots and AI capability.

The [AgTechUCD Agccelerator](https://www.ucd.ie/innovation/agtechucd/agtechucd-agccelerator-programme/) is a 12-week route for agritech, agrifood, veterinary and equine startups in Ireland and Europe. Its criteria include an MVP, traction and fewer than five years in operation. European eligibility means alumni cannot automatically be counted as Irish companies.

## Investors and professional networks

[HBAN](https://www.hban.org/) describes an all-island angel network supported by Enterprise Ireland and InterTradeIreland, with typical pre-seed and seed activity. Its network includes Northern Ireland. The appropriate Atlas relationship is the precise sourced network or programme relationship; it is not a blanket investment edge.

[Frontline Seed](https://frontline.vc/frontline-seed/) focuses on European pre-seed/seed founders and expansion into the US, with B2B experience. [Atlantic Bridge](https://www.abven.com/) describes a cross-border deep-technology strategy spanning Europe, the UK and US. Their stated strategies explain useful research routes, but neither substitutes for an announcement tying a named fund or firm to a particular financing event.

The new Startup Ireland AI programme names ACT Venture Capital, Ada Ventures, Atlantic Bridge, Cherry Ventures, Earlybird, Elkstone, Episode 1, Frontline Ventures, Lightspeed, Playfair, Singular VC and Sure Valley Ventures as its investor network. This is an accelerator-network observation only. Company-specific financing participants are separately retained in `companies.json` and `claims.json`, with the event, source and stated role. Canonical investor IDs remain unresolved unless separately reviewed; fund-manager-brand equivalence is not inferred from a similar name.

## Company research decisions

`company-index.md` enumerates the accepted company profiles and the principal caveats. `companies.json` is the richer research representation, while `startup-records.json` uses the existing Atlas startup schema. A valid projection is ready for owner review, not permission to append it to production.

The evidence preserves renamed companies, acquired businesses, multiple headquarters, historical addresses and explicit unknowns. Conflicting founding years are retained rather than silently reconciled to legal incorporation. Company formation, commercial launch and university spinout can have different dates. A financing press release can also have an announcement dateline that differs from a later publication label.

Amounts retain their original currency and precision. Bare dollar signs are not automatically converted to USD. A secondary sale, acquisition consideration, debt facility, grant, future equity commitment, fund size or cumulative company funding is not treated as a completed primary equity round. Where a current-schema record cannot express these distinctions safely, the richer evidence is held outside its numeric funding fields and the projection map states the omission.
