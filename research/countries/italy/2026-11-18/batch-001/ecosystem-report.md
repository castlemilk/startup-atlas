## Population and cohort boundaries

Italy has at least three materially different observable populations: statutory *startup innovative*, venture investors’ portfolios and the broader set of companies with an Italian operating or founder connection. Atlas should preserve the reason for country inclusion rather than collapse these populations. The [MIMIT statutory description](https://www.mimit.gov.it/it/startup-innovative) requires special-register eligibility and distinguishes innovative startups from innovative SMEs. It also permits some EU/EEA-resident enterprises with an Italian productive site or branch, so registry membership does not itself prove Italian headquarters or incorporation.

The [MIMIT annual 2025 report](https://www.mimit.gov.it/images/stories/documenti/RELAZIONE_ANNUALE_2025_-_17_febbraio_2026_DEF.pdf) records **12,073 registered innovative startups at 31 October 2025**, alongside **3,161 innovative SMEs** in a separate category. These are historical administrative counts. Software and IT services feature prominently, but the report's sector paragraph uses a November date while its section heading and synopsis use October; that discrepancy should remain visible if exact sector counts are later extracted.

The live [ItalyFrontiers landing page](https://startup.registroimprese.it/isin/home) displays **11,940 innovative companies with profiles**, explicitly covering startups and innovative SMEs. Its visible cards are a recently updated subset. This number is neither a current startup-only registry denominator nor the denominator for this research batch. The linked startup-statistics URL resolved with an unresolved template prefix and returned 404. The national universe of Atlas-eligible startups therefore remains **unknown**.

The GDP cohort retains the user's specification: IMF October 2026 WEO, 2026 nominal GDP estimates, Italy position 9 of 30. The [supplied API](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/api/v1/NGDPD/2026) returned a disabled-fetch error, while the [WEO dataset page](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/datasets/WEO) and Italy profile yielded JavaScript placeholders. Indexed excerpts suggest the requested vintage, but the exact value, excluded aggregates and ranking were not independently reproduced. No alternative GDP vintage has been substituted.

## Capital market context and measurement caveats

The [Growth Capital and Italian Tech Alliance Q3 2026 report](https://www.italiantechalliance.com/_files/ugd/ffd03c_66b5ccd8e3584289ae404339c439b049.pdf) reports **€1.477 billion across 210 rounds for January–September 2026**. Its population includes Italian-headquartered companies and foreign-headquartered companies with Italian founders and more than half their employees in Italy. It excludes undisclosed and sub-€50,000 transactions and uses proprietary/confidential inputs. It can infer stages, combine nearby financings and treat an unsplit mixed transaction as equity. Those editorial transformations must not become Atlas facts.

There is a source conflict: the [issuer's announcement](https://growthcapital.vc/index.php/site/news?slug=growth-capital-and-italian-tech-alliance-presenting-the-q3-26-venture-capital-report) quotes €1.465 billion, while its body and the PDF headline give €1.477 billion. The packet preserves both, preferring the PDF only for an explicitly publisher-attributed observation. It does not claim to have reconstructed either amount. The report also references Bending Spoons' public-market transition; lifecycle status needs company or securities evidence before inclusion in a private-startup cohort.

[AIFI's VeM description](https://www.aifi.it/it/venture-capital) provides another useful lens: its public-source monitoring includes informal participants and differs from the association's standard European-method datasets. Aggregates from these series should remain separate unless definitions, periods and transaction coverage have been reconciled.
