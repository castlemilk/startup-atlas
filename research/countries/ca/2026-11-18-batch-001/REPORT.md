## 2. Ecosystem structure and research implications

### Capital is concentrated; the company population is broader

The [CVCA public Q3 2026 market overview](https://intelligence.cvca.ca/reports/q3-2026-vc-pe-canadian-market-overview), released on 10 November, reports approximately **C$4.3 billion across 397 venture deals in January–September 2026**, with Ontario accounting for about **51% of invested capital**, followed by Quebec, British Columbia and Alberta. That is a transaction dataset under CVCA's methodology. It is neither the number of Canadian startups nor a denominator for this research batch. Only the public summary was used; its subscription data was not accessed or reproduced.

[BDC's 2026 venture-capital review](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/analysis-research/canada-venture-capital-landscape) identifies a commercialization and scale-up financing gap, together with dependence on foreign late-stage capital. The practical collection implication is to capture the investor's stated fund or vehicle, domestic/foreign identity where explicitly supported, instrument and transaction status. A headline financing amount alone cannot explain the capital available to a company.

In this selected company set, enterprise AI and applied software intersect with legal workflows, finance, industrial automation, biopharma and energy. Climate companies cover several different activities—emissions measurement, CO2 mineralization, carbon removal and home-energy orchestration—so a single “climate tech” tag would conceal meaningful differences. These are analyst groupings derived from sourced company descriptions; their frequency in this sample is not an estimate of national sector share.
