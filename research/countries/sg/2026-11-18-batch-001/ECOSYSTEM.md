# Singapore startup ecosystem: research checkpoint

**Evidence reviewed: 18 November 2026.** This report accompanies a bounded Startup Atlas research packet. Source identifiers refer to the ecosystem and company source registries; dates describe the cited statement or event, not necessarily a continuously verified present condition. The first checkpoint contains **24 startup candidates and one listed alumnus**, with **57 public founder observations, 36 selected financing events and 468 atomic claims**. Exact file contracts, validation results and integration limits are in [README.md](README.md), [staging/manifest.json](staging/manifest.json) and [coverage.json](coverage.json).

## Scope and the population problem

Singapore is treated here as a research, company-building and regional operating centre. Headquarters, incorporation, programme participation and investor relationships are separate observations. A Singapore office or accelerator affiliation can establish an ecosystem connection without establishing the headquarters or incorporation of the whole group. Nium illustrates the distinction: its company page explicitly identifies co-headquarters in Singapore and San Francisco. This packet retains both rather than forcing a single headquarters country. [Nium company profile](https://www.nium.com/about-us) (`sg-fintech-source-nium-about`).

The session label **GDP rank 28** and the **IMF October 2026 WEO, 2026 nominal GDP estimate** cohort are user-supplied scope. The requested [IMF API](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/api/v1/NGDPD/2026) returned a disabled-reader error, and the [WEO landing page](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/datasets/WEO) yielded a template without country values or a verifiable vintage. Neither rank nor a GDP amount was independently established. These are estimates, not final actuals; no GDP value is supplied here (`sg-gap-imf-api`, `sg-gap-imf-weo`).

Official startup population statements also require qualification. A **5 August 2026 ministry reply** reports more than **4,500 technology startups**, **500 venture capital firms** and **220 incubators and accelerators**. Enterprise Singapore's undated overview has a headline exceeding 4,500 startups, while its Startup SG Network paragraph describes more than 4,000 startups and 400 venture firms, alongside 220 incubators and accelerators. The differing dates and scopes prevent interpreting the difference as measured growth. Neither source supplies a complete named roster with inclusion rules. [Ministry reply](https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/written-reply-to-pq-on-fostering-greater-risk-tolerance-and-strengthening-singapore-s-early-stage-venture-ecosystem/) (`sg-eco-meti`); [Enterprise Singapore overview](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/grow-your-business/partner-with-singapore/innovation-and-startups/overview) (`sg-eco-overview`).

MAS separately reported more than **1,800 fintech firms** on **31 August 2026**. That broader firm population cannot be substituted for a private-startup denominator or added to the technology-startup total. The present batch therefore measures work performed against its own candidate queue, not the fraction of Singapore's startups captured. [MAS announcement](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-commits-s$220-million-to-support-next-phase-of-fintech-innovation) (`sg-eco-mas`).

## Hubs and the research-to-company pathway

**LaunchPad at one-north** is an established startup, incubator and investor cluster near NUS and A*STAR. JTC's **2 March 2026** masterplan describes its continued development and a Kampong AI pilot scheduled from March 2026, with full completion planned for **2028**. The schedule establishes an announced development trajectory; it does not prove completion of each pilot or facility. **Research inference:** proximity between research organisations, venture builders and companies offers a plausible mechanism for repeated founder recruitment, technical collaboration and customer introductions. The source does not quantify the causal contribution of that proximity. [JTC masterplan](https://www.jtc.gov.sg/about-jtc/news-and-stories/press-releases/jtc-unveils-refreshed-masterplan-for-launchpad) (`sg-eco-jtc`).

The same announcement plans a **phased LaunchPad presence in Punggol Digital District from late 2026**, with district-scale testing opportunities in smart cities, fintech, robotics and cybersecurity. This checkpoint has not verified an opening. PDD belongs in the opportunity map as a planned extension, with its announced timing retained at part-of-year precision (`sg-eco-jtc`).

NUSX's current **B71 Singapore** page describes support from pre-seed through Series A, with pathways through **The Hangar** and **LaunchPad at one-north**, plus equipment and laboratory support at Singapore Science Park. The eligibility distinction matters for enumeration: The Hangar requires at least one NUS-affiliated founder but does not require incorporation; the LaunchPad pathway requires Singapore incorporation without requiring NUS affiliation. Participation therefore cannot be interpreted uniformly as an incorporated Singapore startup. The page's inconsistent space and network totals were not adopted. [B71 Singapore](https://nusx.edu.sg/startups/b71/singapore) (`sg-eco-b71`).

NUS's sustainability reporting offers further leads in energy, carbon capture, agrifood and hydrogen. Its narrative is usable as discovery evidence, but rendered counters appeared as zero in the reader and were excluded. Zero rendered text is not evidence of zero activity. [NUS innovation and enterprise reporting](https://sustainability.nus.edu.sg/report/innovation-enterprise/) (`sg-eco-nus-sustainability`).

**National GRIP** supplies a complementary venture-building pathway. NTU's **3 June 2026** account describes a **12-month programme**, jointly run by NUS and NTU with NRF support, and **S$50 million in financial and in-kind programme support**. It reports **30 teams** presenting at Echelon, selected from more than 80 teams that began in January 2025. Those are programme and event populations, not accepted incorporated-company counts. Programme support also remains distinct from startup equity investment. [NTU National GRIP account](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/news/detail/deep-tech-start-ups-showcase-inventions-at-echelon--asia-s-top-tech-and-digital-summit) (`sg-eco-ntu`).

## Public programmes and capital formation

Enterprise Singapore presents **Startup SG** as several support layers. Founder provides mentorship and funding for first-time entrepreneurs through Accredited Mentor Partners. Tech supports proprietary-technology proof of concept and proof of value. Accelerator supports incubators, accelerators and other enablers. Equity encompasses co-investment and fund-of-funds channels, with planned growth-stage deep-tech expansion; Loan connects to government-backed financing through the Enterprise Financing Scheme. This review verifies those purposes, not every eligibility condition, funding cap or current application window. [Startup SG programme overview](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/grow-your-business/partner-with-singapore/innovation-and-startups/join-startup-sg) (`sg-eco-startupsg`).

The same source identifies **Stage One** as a joint Enterprise Singapore and EDB initiative, developed with JTC and managed by ACE.SG, connecting local and international startup communities. **Research inference:** these layers address different obstacles—formation, technical validation, support capacity, financing and market connection. They should be represented as separate programme relationships rather than collapsed into a generic government-funding edge (`sg-eco-startupsg`).

The NRF page establishes the **RIE2030 framework for 2026–2030**, including priorities, flagships and grand challenges. This packet accepts the plan's existence and scope. It does not adopt a budget from the discovered press-release URL, which failed, or from the full handbook, which exceeded the reader's size limit. A known plan and an unverified numerical budget are different evidence states. [NRF RIE framework](https://www.nrf.gov.sg/research-innovation-enterprise/) (`sg-eco-nrf`, with access gaps `sg-gap-nrf-press` and `sg-gap-nrf-handbook`).

For financial technology, MAS's **31 August 2026 FSTI 4.0** announcement commits **S$220 million over three years** across innovation, infrastructure and talent. That is a public scheme commitment, not capital already invested in startups or an amount attributable to each fintech company. Combining it with venture-round totals would misstate financing. [MAS FSTI 4.0 announcement](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-commits-s$220-million-to-support-next-phase-of-fintech-innovation) (`sg-eco-mas`).

## Sector strengths and constraints

The reviewed company examples show substantial variety within fintech. Nium and Thunes supply cross-border payment infrastructure; Aspire combines business finance functions; Funding Societies provides SME financing and payments. bolttech connects insurance distribution participants. Endowus, Syfe and StashAway address wealth and investment services. Coda combines payments with digital-content commerce. These are researcher sector classifications grounded in company descriptions, not a market-share ranking or proof that the examples represent the entire sector. [Thunes](https://www.thunes.com/about-us/) (`sg-fintech-source-thunes-about`); [Aspire](https://aspireapp.com/en-sg/blog/aspire-us-launch) (`sg-fintech-source-aspire-current`); [Funding Societies](https://fundingsocieties.com/about-us) (`sg-fintech-source-funding-societies-about`); [bolttech](https://bolttech.io/who-we-are/) (`sg-fintech-source-bolttech-about`); [Endowus](https://endowus.com/about-us) (`sg-fintech-source-endowus-about`); [Syfe](https://www.syfe.com/about-us) (`sg-fintech-source-syfe-about`); [StashAway](https://www.stashaway.sg/about-us) (`sg-fintech-source-stashaway-about`); [Coda](https://www.coda.co/press/coda-major-payment-institution-license/) (`sg-fintech-source-coda-current`).

Beyond fintech, SGInnovate's **9 September 2025** public release reports **US$371 million across 56 deals in 2024** within its defined early-stage emerging-technology study. It describes stronger activity in advanced manufacturing and sustainability, alongside agrifood constraints involving unit economics and production costs, and biomedical constraints involving capital requirements and late-stage translation talent. These findings concern the report's four-vertical population, not all Singapore venture funding. The underlying full report required login and was not accessed. [SGInnovate study release](https://sginnovate.com/press-room/advanced-manufacturing-and-sustainability-lead-emerging-tech-startup-funding-singapore) (`sg-eco-sginnovate-study`).

**Research inference:** the combination of university venture building, industrial testing infrastructure and investors with explicit enterprise/deep-tech mandates supports prioritising robotics, industrial software, advanced materials, water and energy technologies, and biomedical translation in subsequent enumeration. That prioritisation is a research strategy. The selected sources do not establish the relative size or performance of each sector.

The financing evidence also demonstrates why aggregate amounts require instrument-level review. Endowus's **October 2025** round exceeded **US$70 million**, but comprised **US$20 million fresh capital** plus conversion of existing notes; the fresh-capital instrument is not explicit. Syfe's **June 2025 US$80 million Series C total** includes **US$27 million C1** and **US$53 million C2**. Coda's **April 2022 US$690 million transaction** was a secondary minority-stake sale. Treating these three announcements as equivalent new-cash raises would erase economically significant differences. [Endowus announcement](https://endowus.com/newsroom/endowus-client-assets-cross-10-billion-and-closes-70-million-fundraise-to-scale-wealth-technology-leadership) (`sg-fintech-source-endowus-2025`); [Syfe announcement](https://www.syfe.com/hk/magazine/syfe-raises-us-80-million-series-c-to-power-regional-expansion/) (`sg-fintech-source-syfe-c`); [Coda investor announcement](https://gmo-vp.com/en/news/client-info/2022/04/coda-payments690.html) (`sg-fintech-source-coda-secondary`).

## Investors, accelerators and regional networks

The reviewed investor sources describe overlapping regional mandates. **Vertex Ventures Southeast Asia & India** states an early-stage, sector-agnostic strategy. **Jungle Ventures** describes seed-to-growth investing across Southeast Asia and India. **Wavemaker Partners** emphasises enterprise, deep tech and sustainability while distinguishing Ventures and Growth strategies. **SGInnovate** combines early-stage deep-tech investment with talent and community development. These mandates explain discovery priorities but do not establish Singapore headquarters for every portfolio company. [Vertex](https://www.vertexventures.sg/impact/) (`sg-eco-vertex`); [Jungle](https://www.jungle.vc/portfolio) (`sg-eco-jungle`); [Wavemaker](https://wavemakerpartners.com/) (`sg-eco-wavemaker`); [SGInnovate](https://sginnovate.com/full-portfolio) (`sg-eco-sginnovate-portfolio`).

Enterprise Singapore's current page places **SEEDS Capital under SG Growth Capital**, the Enterprise Singapore–EDB platform, and describes co-investment alongside private venture investors. This current organisational relationship does not justify retrospectively renaming historical EDBI or SEEDS participants in older funding announcements. **SVCA** supplies another network layer, connecting institutional investors, family offices, asset managers and service providers. Association membership establishes neither a particular investment nor a round amount. [SEEDS Capital](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/financial-support/seeds-capital) (`sg-eco-seeds`); [SVCA](https://www.svca.org.sg/history) (`sg-eco-svca`).

Founder-oriented programmes add earlier discovery channels. Antler describes a **six-week Singapore residency** serving Southeast Asia, but its advertised next cohort date, **5 October 2026**, had already passed at retrieval; the next intake remains unknown. Iterative describes **two 12-week Southeast Asian cohorts annually** and publishes an S26 demo-day page. Public company listings are usable leads; gated recordings were not accessed, and a Singapore flag is not accepted headquarters evidence. [Antler Singapore](https://www.antler.co/location/singapore) (`sg-eco-antler`); [Iterative demo day](https://www.iterative.vc/demoday) (`sg-eco-iterative`).

## Evidence limits and the next bounded pass

This checkpoint gives each accepted claim a source, retrieval date and uncertainty state. Company names and domains anchor identity, while founder observations are limited to public professional information. Incorporation of a named operating subsidiary is kept separate from group headquarters and the parent entity. MAS confirms Singapore incorporation for the reviewed Syfe, StashAway, Endowus and Coda operating entities; those observations do not settle each group's holding-company jurisdiction. [Syfe MAS record](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/fid/institution/detail/225171-SYFE-PTE-LTD) (`sg-fintech-source-syfe-mas`); [StashAway MAS record](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/fid/institution/detail/201134-ASIA-WEALTH-PLATFORM-PTE-LTD) (`sg-fintech-source-stashaway-mas`).

The corresponding records for [Endowus Singapore Pte. Ltd.](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/fid/institution/detail/218490-ENDOWUS-SINGAPORE-PTE-LTD) (`sg-fintech-source-endowus-mas`) and [Coda Payments Pte. Ltd.](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/fid/institution/detail/430099-CODA-PAYMENTS-PTE-LTD) (`sg-fintech-source-coda-mas`) are entity-specific evidence. A company suffix, local address or operating licence should not silently substitute for a missing incorporation assertion elsewhere in a group.

**Research inference:** Singapore coverage is best assessed through several separate questions: where decision-making headquarters are located, which legal entity operates locally, how the company connects to research and venture networks, and which markets it serves. A binary country label loses useful information for regional groups. The present evidence supports retaining qualified relationships and unknowns within the existing staging structure; it does not require a new production schema. Likewise, a source with an older dated headquarters statement can support that historical observation without proving the company has not subsequently moved. Refreshing stale evidence and resolving entity boundaries should precede making stronger coverage claims.

Funding announcement dates remain distinct from closing and page-publication dates. Aspire's current Series C page displays **21 August 2025** but carries an announcement dateline of **14 February 2023**. Missing dates, amounts or jurisdictions remain null; portfolio membership is never promoted into a round-participation claim. [Aspire Series C announcement](https://aspireapp.com/en-sg/blog/aspire-closes-us-100m-series-c-round-led-by-lightspeed-and-sequoia-capital-sea) (`sg-fintech-source-aspire-c`).

## Delivery and review boundary

The three company lanes were independently reviewed against 15 source URLs each (with overlap), followed by a projection review. The full registry contains 143 URLs; 118 support retained claims. Eight source reads were blocked, two partial, and one obsolete redirect had no supporting content. One of the 132 accessed sources was excluded after a confidentiality label was observed. No claim uses blocked, partial, redirected or excluded content.

The rich packet separates discovery, source review, unresolved conflicts and production publication. All claims remain unsubmitted to production. Its 17-company next queue is a fixed, resumable worklist, not an invented pagination cursor. English official material dominated the pass; Japanese investor disclosures were read in their available English versions. Chinese, Malay and Tamil discovery has not been systematically covered. Full funding histories, legal group structures, current status/HQ refreshes and national enumeration remain outstanding.
