Four separate conditions

A form, a scope, a clear limit.

All four use the same cobalt focal role. Distinct geometry and explicit labels carry meaning; colour does not create a verdict.

Unknown

Knowledge within scope

The specified fact or evidence condition has not been established within the named research scope.

Form: an open source frame and a separate question hook with a dot.

Use
Introduce a scoped knowledge gap beside the specific question or assessment.
Context
Name the subject and research scope, and identify exactly what has not been established.
Does not mean
The subject is absent, a retrieval failed, or every unreviewed candidate is unknown.

Unavailable

A retrieval attempt

An identified source was not retrievable in a recorded attempt. The condition belongs to that attempt and its context.

Form: a source-side access rail stops before an upright gate, leaving a deliberate gap.

Use
Explain a specific source-access limitation while retaining the source reference.
Context
Identify the source, attempt timestamp and recorded reason for the retrieval failure.
Does not mean
The source was deleted, never existed, or invalidates an earlier captured record.

Stale

A reason to recheck

A dated observation or review needs rechecking under an explicit freshness policy or a recorded review decision.

Form: a separate time dial beside the source frame. Its hands encode no literal time or date.

Use
Explain why freshness needs attention while keeping the dated evidence readable.
Context
Show the observation or review date, the named policy or recheck reason, and evaluation time.
Does not mean
The claim is false or revoked. Age alone supplies no default expiry period.

Reviewed

A scoped assessment

An identified reviewer assessed a specific source, claim or revision under a recorded scope, time and outcome.

Form: an annotation bracket and two short review-note strokes sit beside the source frame.

Use
Introduce a review record and make its assessed scope available to the reader.
Context
Identify the reviewer, subject or revision, assessment scope, review time and recorded outcome.
Does not mean
Universal truth, approval or freshness. A reviewed source can also be stale or unavailable.
